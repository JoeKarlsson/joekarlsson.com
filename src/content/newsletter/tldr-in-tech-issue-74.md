Hey there 😎 Welcome back to another weekly newsletter, packed with juicy knowledge nuggets and served with a side of humor. This week, we're diving into database cryptography, executive networking, mushroom computers, and more.

## [Database Cryptography Fur The Rest Of Us](https://soatok.blog/2023/03/01/database-cryptography-fur-the-rest-of-us/) - Soatok Dreamseeker

tl;dr: This pawsitively awesome 🐾 article defines database cryptography and explains how it manifests in both relational and NoSQL databases. It also explores searchable encryption and offers a case study of MongoDB's Client-Side encryption. Perfect for when you need to keep your data purrivate. 🐈‍⬛

## [Inside The Lab That's Growing Mushroom Computers](https://www.popsci.com/technology/unconventional-computing-lab-mushroom/) - Charlotte Hu

tl;dr: This fungi-tastic article takes us inside the lab where mushroom computers are a thing. Researchers are using mycelium to create binary systems, gates, and even memory. Who knew shrooms could be so brainy? 🍄💡

## [Introducing Service Weaver: A Framework For Writing Distributed Applications](https://opensource.googleblog.com/2023/03/introducing-service-weaver-framework-for-writing-distributed-applications.html) - Srdjan Petrovic, Garv Sawhney

tl;dr: Google's engineering team brings us Service Weaver, an open-source framework for building and deploying distributed applications. Write your app as a modular monolith and deploy it as a set of microservices - like a code transformer! 🤖

## [No-Code Has No Future In A World Of AI](https://www.airplane.dev/blog/no-code-has-no-future-in-a-world-of-ai) - Ravi Parikh

tl;dr: Ravi Parikh, CEO of Airplane, takes a flight of fancy ✈️ by discussing how AI-driven software development will dwarf no-code tools' capabilities and eventually make no-code go the way of the dodo. Sorry, no-code enthusiasts, looks like AI is taking the pilot's seat!

## [Zig And Rust](https://matklad.github.io/2023/03/26/zig-and-rust.html)

_By: Alex Kladov_

tl;dr: Alex Kladov, who has spent seven years working with Rust, now writes Zig full-time. In this post, he shares his personal reasons for the switch. A captivating read for software engineers curious about the Rust-Zig dynamic.

## [Web Stable Diffusion](https://github.com/mlc-ai/web-stable-diffusion)

_By: Unknown_

tl;dr: This GitHub repository features a web-based implementation of the stable diffusion algorithm that runs entirely inside the browser with no server support

## [Move Past Incident Response To Reliability](https://github.com/readme/guides/incident-response)

tl;dr: This guide will help you (1) understand the current standard for incident response and analysis, (2) identify where some teams get themselves in trouble with the current standard, and (3) find your own path through the innovation and dogma of leading a company's approach to reliability. Sounds reliable, doesn't it? 😉

## [How To Contribute To A Project You Have No Idea About](https://grifel.dev/how-to-contribute-without-knowledge) - Michal Warda

tl;dr: In this article, Michal outlines a 5-step process for contributing to a project, even when you're clueless. It's a great reminder that you don't need to know everything to make an impact - just dive in, learn as you go, and make the codebase a better place for everyone. 🏊‍♂️

## [Building Your Executive Network](https://lethain.com/building-exec-network/) - Will Larson

tl;dr: Will shares tactics for engineers to build their executive network, emphasizing the importance of cultivating relationships before you need them. Set a small goal like meeting one new person each month - it's like networking on easy mode! 🎮

That's all for this week, folks! Stay curious and keep coding. Until next week.
