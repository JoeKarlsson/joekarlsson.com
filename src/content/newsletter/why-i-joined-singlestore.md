I am excited to share today that I've joined [SingleStore](https://www.singlestore.com/) (Formerly MemSQL) as a Developer Advocate.

First, I want to say what an honor it is to be joining the founding Community Team in order to help nurture and support the massively talented current and future [SingleStore developers](https://developers.singlestore.com/) as their numbers grow.

But, let's get into it, why did I join SingleStore?

## Innovation.

**It's simple, SingleStore is creating the future of databases.** It's no question that the data demands in every industry are exponentially growing, and I think that SingleStore is uniquely positioned to handle current and future data workloads. Increasingly, the most valuable data workloads are those that are data-intensive. This kind of workload is not compute-bound. It's easy to scale compute in the cloud now. This kind of workload is not storage-bound. Again, it's easy to scale storage in the cloud. Data-intensive workloads are those where \*how\* you process the data becomes the bottleneck.

Take, for instance, a document database used for your SaaS app. Once you start scaling your business, you need analytics and that's the moment when you realize your document database wasn't built for that, so you bolt-on a special-purpose database for analytics, maybe Elasticsearch or Snowflake. Then, you build an API and need low-latency fetches. So, you bolt on another special-purpose database, like Redis or AWS ElastiCache. So on and so forth. You get the idea: folks are building data-intensive apps by stitching together lots of databases and that's just how you had to do it in 2016, but not anymore.

**There's a better way.** SingleStore is the \*single\* database for data-intensive applications. And, it's pretty special. SingleStore seamlessly separates compute from storage. It's the first operational cloud database to do this. This means you can run real-time analytics, update machine learning models on the fly using streaming and historical data without needing to spin up any other systems. SingleStore is currently the only database in the industry that addresses both analytical and transactional workloads for the multi-cloud world.

I think the winners of the great "database wars" are going to be the systems that are the easiest to use, scale/maintain, and affordable, and SingleStore checks all of those boxes.
