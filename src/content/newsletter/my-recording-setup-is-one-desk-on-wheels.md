This week I published [a tour of my recording setup](https://www.joekarlsson.com/blog/recording-setup-tour/). For years my videos were selfies on my iPhone or a webcam in the living room. Then I started recording a lot more video for work at CData, and those needed to look and sound professional. So now a camera, a teleprompter, a mic and two lights all live on one standing desk that rolls.

## One desk, one cable, one sentence

The camera and teleprompter ride on one arm and the mic on another, both clamped to a FlexiSpot standing desk on casters. When I roll the desk two feet to find a better shot, the shot comes with it. When I'm done, I push it back into the corner, and it's a normal desk again.

![My recording desk from the front: an acacia standing desk under kitchen cabinets with a green cutting mat, a gridded softbox on a stand at the left, a mic on a low-profile arm, and a camera behind a teleprompter on a second arm at the right](/images/uses/recording-studio-front.webp)

My laptop plugs into a dock on the desk with one Thunderbolt 5 cable, which gets me the camera, the mic, the prompter screen and charging. Everything on the desk runs off one smart plug. Then I say "I'm recording" to my Home Assistant voice assistant, and the room sets itself up: desk power on, a Hue scene, every media player in the house paused, the TVs off, and the office voice assistant muted so it doesn't wake up while I read a script out loud. It also turns on my 3D printer, though not to print anything. My Prusa has great LEDs, and it looks good glowing on the shelf behind me.

## I got lighting wrong twice

I figured lighting would be easy. A ring light blew my face out. So I went the other way, a dark, moody room full of colored LEDs, and the picture fell apart. With that little light, the camera made up the difference with gain, the picture got noisy and soft, and the compression turned my face to mush.

![Three ungraded stills of me on camera flipping by: August, a webcam in my bright living room lit flat by the window; October 15, a deep blue room with my face dark and soft; October 30, a softbox lighting my face warm and even with the blue room and an orange spotlight behind me](/images/newsletter/my-recording-setup-is-one-desk-on-wheels/lighting.gif)

What worked was treating my face and the mood as two separate problems. A softbox lights my face until the camera gets a clean image, and the mood comes from everything behind me: colored LEDs, the printer, and a cheap spotlight with an orange gel. If you're starting out, light your face first and add the mood after.

## What it cost

About $2,700 at November 2026 prices. The desk and the dock are the two costs most people could skip, if you don't mind plugging in four things and leaving the rig in one spot. The first thing I'd change is the camera arm. I hung a camera and a teleprompter off a mic arm, and even though it's inside the weight rating, it sags.
