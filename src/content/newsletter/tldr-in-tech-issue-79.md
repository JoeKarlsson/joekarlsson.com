## [Ordering Numbers, How Hard Can It Be?](https://orlp.net/blog/ordering-numbers/)

_By: Orson Peters_

tl;dr: Ordering numbers may seem like an easy task, but it can be quite challenging. Even with explicit instructions and guidelines, it is difficult to get it right on the first try. The Python standard library even states that "comparison is pretty much a nightmare." As software engineers, it's important to understand the complexity of seemingly simple tasks and the potential challenges they can present.

## [Contracts You Should Never Sign](https://vadimkravcenko.com/shorts/contracts-you-should-never-sign/)

_By: Vadim Kravcenko_

tl;dr: As a software engineer, there are certain contracts you should avoid signing. These include non-compete clauses in employee contracts, confidentiality agreements, exclusive distribution agreements, and project-based agreements without a clear definition of scope and definition of done. It's important to understand the terms and conditions of any contract before signing it, and to be aware of potential red flags that could negatively impact your career.

## [What We Look For In A Resume](https://huyenchip.com/2023/01/24/what-we-look-for-in-a-candidate.html)

_By: Chip Huyen_

tl;dr: When reviewing software engineering resumes, it's important to look for demonstrated expertise, initiative and persistence, unique perspectives, and impact rather than meaningless metrics. As a hiring manager, it's important to understand that keywords don't necessarily indicate expertise, and to look for evidence of successful projects and meaningful contributions.

## [30-60 Days In A New Leadership Role: Run Experiments For Change](https://larahogan.me/blog/first-60-days-new-role/)

_By: Lara Hogan_

tl;dr: When starting a new leadership role, it's important to run experiments to gather data and make informed decisions. However, it's important to limit the number of experiments to avoid overwhelming employees, and to limit the timeline to 2-3 weeks. It's also important to develop a communication plan and prepare to share the results of the experiments with the team.
