## Don't buy them a server

These are the gifts I'd skip, even the tempting ones:

- **Servers, switches, Wi-Fi access points and NAS drives.** These are the decisions. My network is a UniFi gateway, UniFi access points, and a MikroTik and a UniFi switch, picked so they work together. A surprise router from a different brand goes back in the box.
- **Cloud-only smart home gadgets.** If it needs its own app and its own account to work, a homelabber will either stick it on an isolated network where it can't phone home, or leave it in a drawer.
- **"There's no place like 127.0.0.1" merch.** They already have the shirt.

If you want to get them the big thing they keep talking about, get a gift card and write the thing on the card. More on that in [gifts that aren't hardware](#gifts-for-self-hosters-that-arent-hardware).

## Under $50

### Rackstuds

![Rackstuds DUO in their retail box, black plastic studs that push into square rack holes in place of cage nuts](/images/blog/homelab-gift-ideas-2026/rackstuds.webp)

[Rackstuds](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0CKB8MHMD) replace the cage nuts and screws that come with a server rack. Cage nuts are tiny metal clips you squeeze into square holes with a screwdriver. Rackstuds push in by hand and hold the gear just as well.

I love these. They're the first thing I'd buy for anyone with a rack.

**Ask first:** "Does your rack have square holes?" Rackstuds don't fit round or threaded holes.

### Aqara leak sensors

![A small white round Aqara Water Leak Sensor T1 sitting on a wet floor next to rubber ducks](/images/blog/homelab-gift-ideas-2026/aqara-leak-sensor.webp)

I have four [Aqara water leak sensors](https://www.aqara.com/en/product/water-leak-sensor-t1/) around the house. When one gets wet, my phone gets a critical alert that breaks through Do Not Disturb, and the speakers announce it. I wrote up how that works in [the Home Assistant automations I'd build first](/blog/best-home-assistant-automations/).

They talk Zigbee, and mine pair straight to [Zigbee2MQTT](https://www.zigbee2mqtt.io/devices/SJCGQ12LM.html), so I never installed Aqara's hub or app.

**Ask first:** "Do you run Zigbee?" If they say yes, buy a few. If they say no, pick something else. Without Zigbee they'd need Aqara's own hub and app.

### Self-healing cutting mat

![My recording desk covered by the green WORKLION cutting mat, with a crayon drawing and paintings laid out on it between the mic arm and the camera](/images/uses/recording-studio-front.webp)

A 24x36 [WORKLION cutting mat](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B08Y5R5K5J) covers my studio desk. It's my surface for crafts and hardware projects.

### Mug warmer

![My mug warmer on the corner of my desk, a round black plate holding a red Le Creuset mug, with my pixel art display glowing in the background](/images/blog/homelab-gift-ideas-2026/mug-warmer.webp)

A [warming plate](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B07S7DDY6S) with a 14 oz ceramic mug. It's the least technical thing on my desk.

## $50 to $150

### Home Assistant Voice Preview Edition

![The Home Assistant Voice Preview Edition, a small square gray speaker with a glowing LED ring on top](/images/blog/homelab-gift-ideas-2026/ha-voice-pe.webp)

I swapped every Amazon Alexa in my house for the [Home Assistant Voice Preview Edition](https://www.home-assistant.io/voice-pe/). There are four of them now. It's made by the Home Assistant team, it costs about $69, and the voice processing can run on hardware in the house instead of Amazon's cloud. Mine answers in 2-3 seconds from wake word to spoken reply, because the speech and language models run on a GPU in my rack. On a Raspberry Pi or a small mini PC, expect slower replies; I haven't timed that setup. I wrote up [a fully local voice assistant for Home Assistant](/blog/local-voice-ai-home-assistant-gpu/) if you want the details.

**Ask first:** "Do you run Home Assistant?" Without it this is a speaker that does nothing.

### Brother P-touch CUBE Plus

![The back of my Dell R730 servers with a cable wearing a white P-touch label that reads PRXBOX 2](/images/blog/homelab-gift-ideas-2026/labeled-cable.webp)

Every cable in my rack is labeled, and my [Brother P-touch CUBE Plus](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B07HB8LNSY) did it. It's the best, I love it. It's a Bluetooth label printer you run from a phone app, and it prints on Brother's TZe tape up to 24mm wide.

If they already have a P-touch, a few rolls of TZe tape make a cheap stocking stuffer. Check which tape width their model takes first.

### Elgato Stream Deck

![An Elgato Stream Deck MK.2, a black keypad of 15 colorful LCD buttons on a stand](/images/blog/homelab-gift-ideas-2026/stream-deck.webp)

A [Stream Deck](https://www.elgato.com/us/en/p/stream-deck) is a little keypad of programmable LCD buttons, sold to streamers. I use mine for shortcuts: it mutes and unmutes Zoom, launches my common workflows, and triggers Home Assistant scenes from my desk. The Neo (about $100) is plenty to start; the MK.2 (about $150) has more buttons.

### WiiM Pro

![The WiiM Pro, a small square black music streamer](/images/blog/homelab-gift-ideas-2026/wiim-pro.webp)

The [WiiM Pro](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0BJDY6D1W) plays music in my office. It's cheaper than Sonos or Bose multi-room systems, and in my opinion better integrated with a self-hosted setup: it shows up in Home Assistant and plays from my own music server. I love it.

**Ask first:** "Are you all-in on Sonos?" If they are, they don't want a second ecosystem.

### Raspberry Pi 5

![A clear plastic bin of Raspberry Pis on my shelf with a printed label that reads QUESTIONABLE PIS](/images/blog/homelab-gift-ideas-2026/questionable-pis.webp)

Raspberry Pis are the duct tape of homelabs. In my rack, one Pi is the tiebreaker vote for my two-server cluster, so the servers can tell which one went down, and a Pi also runs a backup copy of my ad-blocking DNS.

Buy the [4GB or 8GB Raspberry Pi 5](https://www.raspberrypi.com/products/raspberry-pi-5/). RAM prices went up all through 2026, and Raspberry Pi [raised the 16GB model to $385](https://www.raspberrypi.com/news/price-rises-for-some-16gb-raspberry-pi-products/) in November 2026. Nobody needs that as a gift. A 4GB or 8GB Pi doesn't have to fit anything they already run, and every homelabber finds a job for one. The board ships alone, so get a kit with a power supply, case and storage, or buy those too.

### Philips Hue Play light bars

![My desk at night with the Hue Play bars lighting the wall behind the two monitors pink](/images/blog/homelab-gift-ideas-2026/desk-hue-play.webp)

Two [Hue Play bars](https://www.bestbuy.com/site/philips-hue-play-smart-led-bar-light-2-pack-white-and-color-ambiance/6298635.p) sit behind my monitors for desk backlighting. They show up in Home Assistant through the Hue bridge.

**Ask first:** "Do you have a Hue bridge?" If they say no, pick something else.

## $150 and up

### Aranet4 Home

![The Aranet4 Home CO2 monitor, a white square with an e-ink screen reading 560 ppm](/images/blog/homelab-gift-ideas-2026/aranet4-home.webp)

An [Aranet4](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B07YY7BH2W) CO2 monitor sits on my desk. When the number climbs, I start getting foggy, so it's my cue to open a window or take a break. Home Assistant [picks it up over Bluetooth](https://www.home-assistant.io/integrations/aranet/), so CO2 can trigger an alert like any other sensor. Mine pushes to my phone when the office stays above 1000 ppm for 5 minutes.

Buy the **Home** version. The Pro has the same sensors and adds support for business base stations nobody needs at home.

### A rack UPS

![My two CyberPower OR1500PFCRT2U rack UPSes stacked on the living room rug, just out of the box](/images/blog/homelab-gift-ideas-2026/ups-unboxed.webp)

I run two [CyberPower OR1500PFCRT2U](https://www.cyberpowersystems.com/product/ups/pfc-sinewave/or1500pfcrt2u/) rack UPSes, battery backups that sit between the wall and the servers. In the summer, the power in my office blips when my air conditioners click on, and the servers ride straight through it. That has saved my ass more times than I can count.

I've never had a long outage drain the battery. If one does, NUT (Network UPS Tools) is set up to watch the UPS and shut the cluster down cleanly, since the UPS doesn't do that part on its own. So far I've always been home to flip the breaker or shut the air conditioners off first. A server that loses power mid-write can corrupt the data on it. I go through what the rest of the rack cost in [my homelab two years later](/blog/homelab-two-years-later/).

**Ask first:** "Is your rack on a UPS yet?" If they say no, have them pick the model. A rack UPS is sized to the power the gear pulls, and they know that number.

### Anker Prime Thunderbolt 5 dock

![The Anker Prime Thunderbolt 5 dock, a silver cube with a blue light ring and ports on the front, with two USB-C cables](/images/blog/homelab-gift-ideas-2026/anker-prime-tb5-dock.webp)

The [Anker Prime Thunderbolt 5 dock](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0DSVVJXK5) replaced my Dell dock. I have one at my main desk and one at my recording station: one cable and the laptop is plugged into everything. It's amazing. It lists at about $400 and goes on sale often. The rest of the desk is in [my development setup](/blog/my-development-setup-2026/).

**Ask first:** "Does your laptop have Thunderbolt?" Every Apple Silicon Mac does, but Macs without Thunderbolt 5 run the dock at Thunderbolt 4 or 3 speed.

For someone on video calls all day, this is the rest of what's on my desk, at roughly $100-400 each: [Sony MDR7506](https://www.amazon.com/Sony-MDR7506-Professional-Diaphragm-Headphone/dp/B000AJIF4E) headphones, the [Elgato Wave:3](https://www.elgato.com/us/en/p/wave-3) mic, an [Elgato Key Light](https://www.amazon.com/gp/product/B07L755X9G), and an [Ergotron LX dual monitor arm](https://www.ergotron.com/en-us/products/product-details/45-245).

## Gifts for self-hosters that aren't hardware

Homelabbers run a lot of free software built by small teams. Paying for it in their name doesn't add anything to their setup:

- **[Home Assistant Link](https://www.nabucasa.com/link/)** (formerly Home Assistant Cloud), $65 a year, if they run Home Assistant. It adds remote access and voice assistant hookups, and the subscription pays for Home Assistant development.

- **A gift card to Micro Center or Amazon**, with a note naming the thing they keep talking about.

## FAQ

### What do you buy someone who has a homelab?

Something that works no matter what they run: desk gear, tools, sensors, or rack accessories like Rackstuds and a UPS. Skip servers, switches, Wi-Fi gear and hard drives. Those depend on choices they've already made, and a wrong brand or size becomes a return. If a gift depends on their setup, text them one yes/no question first.

### What should you not buy a homelabber?

Servers, switches, Wi-Fi access points and NAS drives. Those are decisions they've already made, and a different brand or size than the rest of their gear goes back in the box. Also skip cloud-only smart home gadgets that need their own app and account, since a homelabber wants devices that work locally.

### Is a gift card a lame gift for a homelabber?

No. Homelabbers are picky because their gear has to fit what they already run, and a gift card to a store that sells parts, like Micro Center or Amazon, lets them buy the exact model they've been staring at. Put a note with it that says what it's for. A gift card for "that drive you keep talking about" feels personal.

### What is a good homelab gift under $50?

Rackstuds, the tool-free replacement for cage nuts, if they have a rack with square holes. Aqara leak sensors if they run Zigbee. A self-healing cutting mat for the workbench, or a mug warmer for the desk. All of these are under $50 as of November 2026 and none of them have to match a brand they already picked.

### What is a good gift for someone just starting a homelab?

A Raspberry Pi 5 with 4GB or 8GB, or a small mini PC like the Beelink EQ14 (Intel N150), which I haven't used myself. Someone without a lab hasn't made the decisions a gift could clash with, so this is the one case where buying the server works. Skip the 16GB Pi 5: 2026 RAM prices pushed it to about $385.

### What smart home gifts work with Home Assistant?

The Home Assistant Voice Preview Edition is made by the Home Assistant team and runs voice locally. Aqara leak sensors work over Zigbee, and the Aranet4 CO2 monitor has a built-in Home Assistant integration over Bluetooth. A Home Assistant Link subscription ($65 a year, formerly Home Assistant Cloud) helps pay for Home Assistant development.
