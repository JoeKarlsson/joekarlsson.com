I cried twice at a movie where Olivia Colman marries a basket.

![At a candlelit altar, the Wicker Husband's dark woven hands reach for the Fisherwoman's hand, which holds a small bunch of white flowers against her green dress.](/images/reviews/wicker/wedding-woven-hand-takes-hers.webp)

_The wedding, from Black Bear's official trailer for Wicker._

I'm not an expert in monster romance. I can definitely appreciate a horny movie, though, and Wicker charmed me way more than I expected. It's so much sweeter and lighter than what I'm used to. Both times I cried, it was over Olivia Colman's Fisherwoman not believing she's worthy of being loved like this.

Wicker is an indictment of our modern society, and I love that it doesn't crank things up to 11 the way [The Handmaid's Tale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Handmaid%27s_Tale_%28TV_series%29) does with a fascist patriarchy. It takes our exact culture and twists it a little. Instead of exchanging wedding rings, couples exchange wedding collars.

![In a stone chapel lit by a tall window and candles, a smiling young groom fastens a small metal-clasped collar around the throat of his veiled bride while a man in a dark coat and hat looks on.](/images/reviews/wicker/groom-locks-wedding-collar.webp)

_A village wedding, from the official trailer._

The men are defined solely by their jobs: the Butcher, the Baker, the Innkeeper. The women are referred to by their husbands' jobs, and the one woman named for her own trade is the one the whole village mocks.

![A gray-haired villager kneels in the dirt with a hand on his chest and a jug under his arm, hamming it up for the Fisherwoman, seen from behind in her shawl and hat, while the Tailor's Wife (Elizabeth Debicki) leans on a post with her arms crossed and a smirk.](/images/reviews/wicker/villager-mocks-fisherwoman.webp)

_From the official trailer._

Taking our own traditions and spinning them a half turn is such an interesting way to get people to rethink the originals.

Into this village walks [the Wicker Husband](https://www.goodreads.com/book/show/13250089), woven by Peter Dinklage's Basket Weaver, and he does everything the men around him aren't doing for their wives. He fixes the roof, tends the garden and mends the bed they broke on their wedding night. Alexander Skarsgård plays him through a mask, with nothing but his eyes, a little bit of his mouth and a very stiff body, and it works.

![The Wicker Husband, his whole body woven from wicker down to the beard and eyebrows, strides stiffly toward the camera through a sunlit forest, arms held away from his sides.](/images/reviews/wicker/wicker-husband-stiff-walk.webp)

_From Black Bear's official Wicker ASMR video._

The village greets him with fear and jealousy, and the husbands sulk at the inn. He should be an inspiration to them.

It's a simple fairy tale, a very funny, sweet, charming popcorn film, and it's extremely effective. A story this digestible can change mainstream opinions in a way a sloggy, trauma-filled nightmare romance never could. I'm curious how it goes over with normies, and I'm very interested to see the conservative response. I'd wholeheartedly recommend it, especially to romance lovers.

I've been trying to think of another film like this one, and I can't. The closest I get is [Twilight](/reviews/the-twilight-saga-new-moon/), and in that kind of monster romance the woman must be punished for wanting pleasure. Bella's half-vampire pregnancy nearly kills her. In Wicker, society judges the Fisherwoman for wanting him, and Colman plays her as an older horny woman with something missing inside of her, unibrow and all. The movie never punishes her for it. The punishment goes to the Butcher, who keeps his young wife locked in the basement, wins the blackthorn bride of his own.

![A gaunt, hollow-eyed Bella propped up in bed next to an IV drip in Breaking Dawn Part 1.](/images/reviews/wicker/bella-breaking-dawn-then-fisherwoman-1.webp 'spoiler') ![The Fisherwoman at a village dance, eyes closed and smiling up at the sky, with the Wicker Husband's woven hand on her head.](/images/reviews/wicker/bella-breaking-dawn-then-fisherwoman-2.webp 'spoiler')

_Breaking Dawn Part 1, from the official trailer, next to Wicker, from Black Bear's final trailer._

What actually threatens the marriage is the Fisherwoman's own doubt. The Tailor's Wife (Elizabeth Debicki) plants a rumor, and the Fisherwoman believes it, because some part of her never believed someone like him would choose her. Her hurt and abandonment and skepticism are where I lost it. I think all of us struggle with believing we're worthy enough to be loved by someone like the Wicker Husband.

We should strive for that kind of love, reach for it, and not put up with anything less.
