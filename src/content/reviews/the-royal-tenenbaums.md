This is the Wes Anderson movie that gets me every time.

![Hands on a library copy of a book called The Royal Tenenbaums, a green curtain and two candles on its cover, next to a date stamp on an ink pad.](/images/reviews/the-royal-tenenbaums/library-book-opening.webp)

This time it got me through Chas. I first saw The Royal Tenenbaums at a foundational age, and it's been my favorite Anderson ever since. But this year I've been feeling a lot of anger at my parents, and I needed to see a movie about adult children dealing with an emotionally negligent, distant father.

I really resonated with Chas this time. I think he's the heart of the movie, and the only character who really grows. He's angry the whole way through, at a father who shot him with a BB gun when he was a kid and left the BB in his hand, and Ben Stiller plays that anger very well.

![Royal, in a hooded parka and sunglasses, fires a BB gun from a rooftop next to a shingled turret.](/images/reviews/the-royal-tenenbaums/royal-shoots-chas-bb-gun.webp)

You can see where that anger went. The boy who ran a business out of his bedroom in a suit grows up to put his sons in matching red tracksuits and time their fire drills with a stopwatch.

![Young Chas in a gray suit and tie, on the phone behind a desk with a computer and a coffee maker.](/images/reviews/the-royal-tenenbaums/young-chas-suit-then-red-tracksuits-1.webp) ![Grown Chas in a red tracksuit hauling silver suitcases and sleeping bags, one of his sons beside him in the same tracksuit.](/images/reviews/the-royal-tenenbaums/young-chas-suit-then-red-tracksuits-2.webp)

![Chas in a red tracksuit stands between his two sons in pajamas on a dark street at night, holding a stopwatch.](/images/reviews/the-royal-tenenbaums/chas-fire-drill.webp)

||At the end, Royal finally tells him, "Sorry I let you down, Chas. All of you."|| Chas says, "I've had a rough year, Dad." Royal says, "I know you have, Chassie." Chas gets to say it and be heard.

![Chas and Royal, both in gray suits, crouch over a dalmatian on the sidewalk beside a crashed car.](/images/reviews/the-royal-tenenbaums/rough-year-dad.webp 'spoiler')

![Royal puts a hand on Chas's shoulder, and Chas looks up at him.](/images/reviews/the-royal-tenenbaums/i-know-you-have-chassie.webp 'spoiler')

With as much anger at my own parents as I've got right now, it felt so good to watch his go somewhere and finally resolve.

I think the apology lands because Royal is a real son of a bitch, and that's Gene Hackman. He didn't want the part, he was [furious about the pay](https://www.yahoo.com/entertainment/articles/wes-anderson-recalls-gene-hackman-163337695.html), and [by Bill Murray's account](https://deadline.com/2025/02/bill-murray-remembers-gene-hackman-tough-nut-wes-anderson-1236305025/) he was "really rough" on his director. That pushback is the alchemy. Anderson's movies are dollhouses, and Royal is the one thing in this one that won't sit where he's put, which makes it the most human film he's made.

The prologue tours the house room by room, each one labeled by floor like a dollhouse, and Hackman walks right out of a shot lined up on his son's trophies.

![An open door with a UPS pick-up sign under the title Chas' Room (2nd Floor).](/images/reviews/the-royal-tenenbaums/dollhouse-room-then-royal-out-of-frame-1.webp) ![Royal blurring out the right side of a shot framed on a wall of trophies.](/images/reviews/the-royal-tenenbaums/dollhouse-room-then-royal-out-of-frame-2.webp)

When he finally gets the grandsons out of that house, they ride the back of a garbage truck.

![Royal and his two grandsons, in red tracksuits and sunglasses, hang off the back of a garbage truck on a sunny street.](/images/reviews/the-royal-tenenbaums/royal-and-grandsons-on-garbage-truck.webp)

It still touches the themes he keeps coming back to, and every piece of it just fucking works.

It's still his hardest-hitting film for me.
