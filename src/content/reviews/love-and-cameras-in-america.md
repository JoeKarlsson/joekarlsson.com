A couple of people in this documentary have spent 17 years thanking God it never got a release.

[Nobody would distribute it](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Love_and_Cameras_in_America), so it sat on a shelf until WildBrain [put the whole thing up on YouTube](https://rollingstonephilippines.com/?p=90661) this fall. I'm a turbo Nathan Fielder fan, and it was so wonderful seeing his blueprint for [You Can See Everything](/reviews/you-can-see-everything/).

![A 24-year-old Nathan Fielder in an olive polo outside a parking garage in 2008.](/images/reviews/love-and-cameras-in-america/nathan-2008.webp 'Love and Cameras in America · 2008')
![Nathan with gray in his hair and headphones around his neck on a beach-house couch in You Can See Everything.](/images/reviews/love-and-cameras-in-america/nathan-you-can-see-everything.webp 'You Can See Everything')

_Nathan in 2008, and 17 years later in You Can See Everything._

It was also a throwback. In 2008 I was a college freshman, it was the first election I was old enough to vote in, and I sank deep into both candidates. So it's funny to watch a Canadian wander around D.C. and Virginia completely failing to understand American cultural politics.

Nathan's subject has always been how people behave with a camera pointed at them, and it starts here. He sets out to make a serious documentary about the election, gets more uneasy with every street interview, gives up on politics, and ends up thinking he's falling in love with a beautiful aspiring actor named Ashley.

![Nathan in the back seat of a car, reading a script.](/images/reviews/love-and-cameras-in-america/car-rehearsal-nathan.webp)
![Ashley at the wheel, laughing.](/images/reviews/love-and-cameras-in-america/car-rehearsal-ashley.webp)

_Nathan running Ashley's lines with her on the way to the zoo._

It's much more meandering than You Can See Everything, and it has a lot less to say about how we present ourselves on camera.

Nathan for You took the idea in one direction: how far he can push people into doing uncomfortable things because a camera is in front of them. It also had the advantage of being shot in Los Angeles, a town full of people who will literally do anything on camera.

![Nathan in a green Dumb Starbucks apron, his back to the camera, facing a crowd of photographers and TV cameras in a Los Angeles parking lot.](/images/reviews/love-and-cameras-in-america/dumb-starbucks-crowd.webp)

_Nathan for You's Dumb Starbucks press conference._

You Can See Everything made it a back and forth. Nathan tests what he can get Elizabeth Holmes and Billy Evans to do, and they work on him right back, steering how they end up portrayed.

Love and Cameras is somewhere in between, in a more immature form. The people he stops in D.C. in 2008 are warier and savvier, and a lot less interested in the fame a documentary crew can bring.

![Nathan in profile at a gas station, with a man in a blue shirt pumping gas behind the pump and the price sign reading 3.19.](/images/reviews/love-and-cameras-in-america/gas-station.webp)

_The man at the pump who didn't have time for a question about gas prices._

He still finds the ones who want it: an Austin Powers impersonator who barges into an interview outside the White House, a former lobbyist who now sings political satire, and, mostly, Ashley.

![Nathan in a rain jacket outside the White House fence as a man in a blue velvet Austin Powers suit, frilly cravat and thick glasses waves his way into the shot.](/images/reviews/love-and-cameras-in-america/austin-powers.webp)

_The Austin Powers impersonator, arriving._

Even in skeptical 2008 D.C., these people are everywhere.

When Ashley mentions that her pearl necklace was a Christmas gift from her longtime boyfriend, Nathan gets out of the car and ends shooting for the day. He sees her once more and says she was "more acting like Ashley rather than just being herself."

A year later he still couldn't say whether she liked him or liked being filmed: ["Maybe it's romantic, and maybe it's not."](https://www.thecoast.ca/arts-music/playing-the-fielder-1275758/) You Can See Everything spends three hours asking the same thing about Elizabeth Holmes.

It's a fun snapshot of America right as millennials were coming of age, and of a fun time in my life. After [Democrats swept the 2026 midterms](https://www.wpxi.com/news/trending/election-2026-democrats-capture-both-houses-congress/5ILOWTVABVDPZFYP7VBBCX4EWE/), I'm starting to feel that hope again, and this was a nice reminder of the last time I felt true hope in an American election.

Stray thoughts:

- Early Nathan is somehow even more nervous and awkward than the Nathan we got later.
