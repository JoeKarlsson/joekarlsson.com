Fjord wants to be [Anatomy of a Fall](/reviews/anatomy-of-a-fall/) and keeps forgetting to leave the jury anything to argue about.

I went in wanting that argument, and Fjord should have been an easy sell. I've pleaded with somebody at the DMV to just help me, human to human. I've cried on the phone to an insurance company, begging them to cover a medical claim. I've met plenty of stern, power-hungry people in tiny positions of power who will wield the rules at any cost, and I can't stand them. Put one of them in a Norwegian child welfare office and I'm already halfway to your side.

![A fjord village under steep, misty mountains: red boathouses, white wooden houses and a small marina with a few boats.](/images/reviews/fjord-2026/fjord-village.webp)
_The village on the fjord where the Gheorghius move._

Cristian Mungiu barely has to try. A Romanian engineer (Sebastian Stan) and his Norwegian wife (Renate Reinsve) move their five kids to her tiny hometown on a fjord, a teacher spots bruises on their oldest daughter, and this devout evangelical family ends up fighting the movie's version of a godless progressive majority, one that treats a few smacks on the butt as abuse. Two and a half hours later, I was bored, and I think that's the movie's problem more than mine.

![In a school gym full of kids on blue mats, a teacher in a pale tracksuit jacket studies the back of a girl with a long braid.](/images/reviews/fjord-2026/bruises-in-gym-class.webp)
_Gym class, where the bruises get spotted._

Fjord stacks the deck. The teacher who spots the bruises also works for Child Welfare Services, and she's already flagged the family once, because their seven-year-old told a classmate who'd said she was a lesbian that she'd go to hell.

![The teacher, in a beige cardigan, testifies at a hearing while the family's lawyer stands facing her with a green folder. Subtitle: Judith Gheorghiu said it was a great sin.](/images/reviews/fjord-2026/great-sin-testimony.webp)
_The teacher on the stand at the hearing._

So when she reports the bruises, it reads like a grudge. The family's lawyer gets to float a language barrier and kids roughhousing in gym class. Every Norwegian with a clipboard is a straw man, set up to fall over. And Mungiu [told TIME](https://time.com/article/2026/10/23/fjord-true-story/) he made it from a "neutral perspective," as "a mediator."

The rigging is what makes it boring. Anatomy of a Fall had me leaning forward because I didn't know what happened. Fjord tells me who to root for in the first act and then makes me sit there for two more hours.

I don't need a movie to tell the truth. I loved [You Can See Everything](/reviews/you-can-see-everything/), and I don't mind that it lies to tell a bigger story. Bending the truth while claiming you're just asking questions is different. It's a bit disingenuous, and it left me cold.

Some of it does land. Kids lie; I was one, I'd know. Progressives have their prejudices too, and the film is clear about that. Pulling five kids out of a home is an extreme thing to do. I'm not a parent, and I still don't think even a light slap or the threat of one counts as parenting.

![Sebastian Stan as Mihai sits at a desk in a police office while an officer and an investigator stand over him. Subtitle: That's a matter of interpretation. A slap on the butt is not "hitting".](/images/reviews/fjord-2026/slap-is-not-hitting.webp)
_Mihai with the police._

I suspect the right and the left are going to walk out of this with two different movies in their heads, which is kind of funny.

The ending is where Mungiu's thumb on the scale is easiest to see: ||the lawyer who argued for the state congratulates the remaining families and walks out, someone with a good job and no real stake in any of it.|| It's the one moment that felt true to every phone call I've made to an insurance company.

Fjord didn't change my mind about anything. It mostly reminded me how much I hate the DMV. I wanted Anatomy of a Fall.

Stray thoughts:

- Sebastian Stan gives an incredible performance as Mihai, and his shaved head looks great.
