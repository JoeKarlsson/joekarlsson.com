Scorsese made two hours about Catholic guilt, and I'm the one who walked out feeling like I'd done penance.

I came to this one straight from [Kundun](/reviews/kundun/). The whiplash was wild: one religion's slow, still meditation, then another's screaming ambulance tearing through Hell's Kitchen.

Bringing Out the Dead is a super Catholic movie. Even the hospital is named for the Virgin Mary.

![Nicolas Cage's face lit red in the dark.](/images/reviews/bringing-out-the-dead/our-lady-of-perpetual-mercy-1.webp) ![The hospital's sign, Our Lady of Perpetual Mercy Hospital.](/images/reviews/bringing-out-the-dead/our-lady-of-perpetual-mercy-2.webp)

Nicolas Cage plays Frank Pierce, a burned-out paramedic working three night shifts, haunted by a girl he couldn't save and desperate for someone to forgive him, and every call plays like another station of the cross.

![A young girl, Rose, lit gold against the dark, staring back at the camera.](/images/reviews/bringing-out-the-dead/roses-ghost.webp)

Faith and guilt are the parts of Scorsese I've always struggled to connect with, and here I just don't care. Guilt isn't something I think about, and it isn't something I think is important. From the outside, the guilt Catholics are raised with looks like an undue mental burden, put there to scare people into staying faithful to the church and to God, and to keep the donations coming.

Take away the guilt and the night is just [After Hours](/reviews/after-hours/) again. Same manic, New-York-as-purgatory energy, same guy who can't get out of the city until morning, but longer, more repetitive, less funny, and missing the existential philosophy of After Hours that I fucking loved.

![Griffin Dunne soaked and lost on a rainy street in After Hours.](/images/reviews/bringing-out-the-dead/after-hours-again-1.webp 'After Hours · 1985') ![Nicolas Cage behind a rain-streaked ambulance window.](/images/reviews/bringing-out-the-dead/after-hours-again-2.webp 'Bringing Out the Dead · 1999')

_Griffin Dunne in Park Circus's 4K restoration trailer for After Hours, and Cage here._

Frank works three night shifts, and they blur into one long, repetitive one. Same seat, same windshield, a different partner each night.

![Three frames of Frank in the ambulance cab at night: with John Goodman, with Ving Rhames, then with Tom Sizemore.](/images/reviews/bringing-out-the-dead/three-nights-three-partners.webp)

I was confused about the tone the whole way through. Scorsese is clearly showing a horrible night with horrible people, and he lays a rip-roaring Scorsese rock and roll soundtrack over it, plus a weird bit of jazz that sounded like New Orleans to me, in a movie set in New York. I kept asking myself whether a scene was supposed to be funny. Is this a joke? It just didn't work for me. John Goodman and Ving Rhames, as two of Frank's partners, are the only times I knew for sure, and they're the standouts. They bring some levity to it.

![John Goodman talking at the wheel of the ambulance.](/images/reviews/bringing-out-the-dead/goodman-and-rhames-1.webp) ![Ving Rhames leading a crowd in a nightclub.](/images/reviews/bringing-out-the-dead/goodman-and-rhames-2.webp)

Cage is manic, but still restrained by Nicolas Cage standards, which says a lot. He's also the least attractive I've ever seen him. It is a dank, wet, purple performance.

![Nicolas Cage in a hospital, pale and sweating under blue light, staring.](/images/reviews/bringing-out-the-dead/dank-wet-purple.webp)

And it's genuinely beautiful. The manic camera speed-ups, the whips and pans, all those visual flourishes: classic Scorsese.

![The camera on the ambulance roof in fast motion, tearing down a wet avenue at night with the city lights smearing past.](/images/reviews/bringing-out-the-dead/speed-up.webp)

It still didn't save the movie for me, and I served my two hours anyway.

I did not care for this film. I will not be revisiting it.

Stray thoughts:

- Judy Reyes, Carla from Scrubs, plays an ICU nurse here in 1999, and she's playing Carla again in the 2026 Scrubs revival. It's crazy how long she's been typecast as a nurse: Wikipedia lists three different nurses across 27 years.

![Three frames of Judy Reyes in scrubs: a close-up as the ICU nurse in Bringing Out the Dead, Carla pushing a gurney in Scrubs, and Carla in floral scrubs beside a patient in the revival.](/images/reviews/bringing-out-the-dead/judy-reyes-nurse-1999-2001-2026.webp)

_Judy Reyes as the ICU nurse here (1999), as Carla in NBC's 2001 Scrubs promo, and as Carla in ABC's 2026 Scrubs trailer._

- Bringing Out the Dead has a lot of Sopranos overlap. Aida Turturro, who'd become Janice Soprano the next year, plays Nurse Crupp, and the soundtrack felt very Sopranos to me. The influence probably runs the other way, though: Scorsese finished shooting this three days before The Sopranos premiered, and David Chase has named Goodfellas as one of the show's influences.
