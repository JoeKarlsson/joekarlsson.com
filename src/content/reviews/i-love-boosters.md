Not since [Sinners](/reviews/sinners-2025/) have I seen a movie so pro pussy eating.

![In Sinners, a woman holds a man's face in both hands as he tips his head back against a weathered wall.](/images/reviews/i-love-boosters/sinners-then-i-love-boosters-1.webp 'Sinners · 2025') ![In I Love Boosters, a woman with blond curls lies back on a pillow, eyes half closed, in red light.](/images/reviews/i-love-boosters/sinners-then-i-love-boosters-2.webp 'I Love Boosters · 2026')

_Sinners, from Warner Bros.' [trailer](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7joulECTx_U), and I Love Boosters._

I love Boots Riley so much. For those unfamiliar, Riley is the frontman of [The Coup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Coup), a communist hip-hop group from Oakland, and one of the only openly communist filmmakers working in American cinema right now.

![Boots Riley, in a black leather jacket, speaking into a microphone over a protest banner.](/images/reviews/i-love-boosters/boots-riley-mlk-day-rally-2016.webp)

_Riley at a Martin Luther King Jr. Day rally in Emeryville, California, 2016. Photo: [Pax Ahimsa Gethen](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Boots_Riley_20160118-1099.jpg), [CC BY-SA 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/4.0/)._

[Sorry to Bother You](/reviews/sorry-to-bother-you/) was his directorial debut - a film that took years to get made because Hollywood doesn’t fund explicitly anti-capitalist work. In 2026, it’s still rare to see a wide release studio film with genuinely leftist politics. Not liberal. Leftist. I Love Boosters is that rare thing.

This is a sprawling film that covers capitalism, fashion, race, community - a leftist heist movie with clear visual inspiration from Michel Gondry’s playbook.

![Three women in matching mustard-yellow store uniforms and wigs, one with big curls, one with a red bob, one blond in round yellow glasses, standing in a yellow boutique.](/images/reviews/i-love-boosters/crew-undercover-at-metro-designers.webp)

_The crew, undercover as Metro Designers clerks._

The surrealism, the handmade aesthetic, the way ideas become literal on screen.

![A laptop showing a store's security feed, where a woman in a white jacket holds open a small black bag and clothes from the racks around her fly into it.](/images/reviews/i-love-boosters/magic-bag-on-the-security-feed.webp)

_On the security feed, one woman empties a whole boutique into one small bag._

It reminded me of [One Battle After Another](/reviews/one-battle-after-another/) - revolutionary Black women fighting back - but this is a more openly comedic, colorful, surreal version of that energy.

I will be seated for any Boots Riley film or show. I loved [I’m a Virgo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/I%27m_a_Virgo), loved Sorry to Bother You. I was ready for any strange zags that were gonna be thrown at me, and I’m so glad I was here for it. Soul sucking pussy succubuses, demons, vampires, walking claymation, muscle suits.

![A skinless figure made of glistening red muscle, lit red and blue, runs through a dark street holding out a pistol.](/images/reviews/i-love-boosters/stop-motion-flayed-man-with-a-gun.webp)

_One of the stop-motion flayed men, animated by Mystery Meat._

This film is lowbrow and high ideal, with creative energy bursting out of the literal seams.

![A yellow delivery van tips up a steep model street at night, a police tank behind it.](/images/reviews/i-love-boosters/miniature-van-chase.webp)

_The getaway, shot on a miniature San Francisco. Riley [told American Cinematographer](https://theasc.com/article/i-love-boosters-interview/), “So, I reasoned, as we were on a miniature stage, let’s make this fun.”_

The fashion stuff is particularly smart.

![In a wig shop with a turquoise wall, a woman in an Oakland T-shirt explains something with both hands, with the subtitle "That's dialectical materialism."](/images/reviews/i-love-boosters/thats-dialectical-materialism.webp)

_The wig shop where someone finally explains what the gadget does._

The film reflects both sides - how the fashion industry is a tool of capitalism, but also how fashion can be a way to fight oppression. It draws a clear line between fashion as personal expression and art versus fashion as consumption.

![Demi Moore as the designer Christie Smith, blond bob and black glasses, under the big red S in her studio.](/images/reviews/i-love-boosters/christie-smith-then-the-boosters-dressed-up-1.webp) ![Four women in a parking lot in clothes blown up into huge ruffles and flowers.](/images/reviews/i-love-boosters/christie-smith-then-the-boosters-dressed-up-2.webp)

_Christie Smith’s studio, and street clothes after a blast from the accelerator._

The industry grinds people down. The clothes themselves can still be resistance.

For how surreal this is, it might be the most realistic movie I’ve watched all year. The politics feel true. The community feels true. The way capitalism grinds people down feels true.

This movie isn’t perfect, and I don’t think it’s trying to be. But it has lofty ideals, goals, and values that it’s expressing in a wildly creative way. If you’re into fashion, see this. If you’re into collective action, see this. If you’re into wild surreal sci-fi, see this. If you like color, see this. It’s a reminder that fashion, art, friends, and community can be used to fight back against capitalism.

I am ecstatic this got a wide release, and I’m curious to see the popular reaction. It’s a weird one. Lots of in-universe rules to set up. But it’s so worth it.
