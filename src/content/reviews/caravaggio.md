This is what a biopic should be: not the facts of a life but the fever of one.

![Jerusaleme, in a black jacket, bent over a bare wooden table in a whitewashed room while Caravaggio lies in the narrow bed behind him.](/images/reviews/caravaggio/jerusaleme-at-the-table-caravaggio-in-bed.webp)

I don't need a beat-for-beat march through an artist's years. I want the feeling of it, the heat coming off it, and Jarman gives you exactly that. Every frame is composed like one of Caravaggio's own paintings, all candlelight and shadow, less a biography than a baroque canvas that happens to move.

![Sean Bean as Ranuccio, bare-chested in a white loincloth and headband, posing with a sword in one hand and the other arm stretched out, lit from above against black.](/images/reviews/caravaggio/ranuccio-as-the-executioner-then-the-martyrdom-of-saint-matthew-1.webp 'Caravaggio · 1986') ![Caravaggio's painting of a nearly naked executioner with a sword standing over the fallen Saint Matthew as onlookers recoil.](/images/reviews/caravaggio/ranuccio-as-the-executioner-then-the-martyrdom-of-saint-matthew-2.webp 'Caravaggio, The Martyrdom of Saint Matthew · 1599–1600')

_Ranuccio posing as the executioner, and Caravaggio's The Martyrdom of Saint Matthew (1599–1600), San Luigi dei Francesi, Rome. Painting: Caravaggio, [public domain](<https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:The_Martyrdom_of_Saint_Matthew-Caravaggio_(c._1599-1600).jpg>)._

![Dexter Fletcher as the young Caravaggio, bare-shouldered and sweaty, a crown of vine leaves around his head against a green wall.](/images/reviews/caravaggio/young-caravaggio-as-bacchus-then-the-self-portrait-1.webp 'Caravaggio · 1986') ![Caravaggio's self-portrait as a pale, sickly Bacchus in a vine-leaf wreath, holding a bunch of grapes.](/images/reviews/caravaggio/young-caravaggio-as-bacchus-then-the-self-portrait-2.webp 'Caravaggio, Young Sick Bacchus · c. 1593')

_Dexter Fletcher as the young Caravaggio, and Caravaggio's self-portrait as the Young Sick Bacchus (c. 1593), Galleria Borghese, Rome. Painting: Caravaggio, [public domain](<https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Young_Sick_Bacchus-Caravaggio_(1593).jpg>)._

The typewriter, the pocket calculator, the motorbike, the electric bar lights, and, most of all, the fact that Caravaggio himself dressed his biblical figures in the contemporary clothes of his own day. He dragged sacred time into the now.

![Caravaggio's painting of Christ pointing across a dark room at a table of men in feathered caps, slashed sleeves and hose, counting coins.](/images/reviews/caravaggio/calling-of-saint-matthew-painting.webp)

_Caravaggio's The Calling of Saint Matthew (1599–1600), San Luigi dei Francesi, Rome. Painting: Caravaggio, [public domain](<https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:The_Calling_of_Saint_Matthew-Caravaggo_(1599-1600).jpg>)._

Jarman just runs the same play four centuries later.

![A dim tavern strung with colored electric light bulbs, a waiter in a long white apron among the tables, a drunk asleep face-down on one.](/images/reviews/caravaggio/tavern-under-electric-bar-lights.webp)

The modern junk doesn't break the spell, it's how he tells a truer thing about the man than any correct date could.

And god, it's gay. Properly, unapologetically, 1986-and-does-not-care gay, which you almost never got then and barely get now. The whole thing is Caravaggio on his deathbed remembering the two people he wanted, the street thug Ranuccio and Ranuccio's mistress Lena, a bisexual triangle where desire, class, art, and violence are all the same hunger. More of this. More bi men on screen who want everyone in the room. More, more, more.

It floors me that this was pulled off in a London studio for pocket change and still looks like the Louvre bleeding.

A dying man, a room full of everyone he desired, and the light doing all the talking.

Stray thought: Swinton is so young here I stopped the film to check the credits. Her debut, the very start of her and Jarman, and she already has the face that gives absolutely nothing away.
