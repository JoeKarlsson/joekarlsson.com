A film about a lonely movie nerd who snaps and goes on a killing spree dressed as classic Hollywood icons. I've never felt more seen. Minus the murders.

Fade to Black is a 1980 oddity that somehow made $15 million on a $1.8 million budget and then vanished into cult obscurity. Dennis Christopher - fresh off his Oscar-adjacent turn in Breaking Away - plays Eric Binford, a shy cinephile who works at a film distribution warehouse, lives with his nagging aunt, and gets bullied by his coworkers. One of those coworkers is a young Mickey Rourke, already radiating menace in a bit part. When Eric gets stood up by a Marilyn Monroe lookalike he's been obsessing over, something snaps. He starts dressing as Dracula, the Mummy, Hopalong Cassidy, and Cody Jarrett from White Heat - and killing the people who wronged him.

The kills are shot in the style of the films that inspired them. When Eric becomes Cagney, the movie becomes a gangster picture. When he's Dracula, it's Gothic horror. That's a genuinely clever touch, and director Vernon Zimmerman - who insisted this wasn't meant to be a horror film, whatever that means - brings a dreamlike quality to Eric's descent that keeps the whole thing from feeling exploitative.

As a film nerd, this one was a joy. The movie is packed with clips, posters, and references - everything from White Heat to 99 River Street to The Prince and the Showgirl. It's a love letter to classic Hollywood that also happens to be a slasher. The finale takes place at Mann's Chinese Theater. Of course it does.

I'm surprised there hasn't been a remake. Then I thought about the rights clearance for all those film clips and understood immediately. The licensing alone would be a fucking nightmare. Still - someone should try.
