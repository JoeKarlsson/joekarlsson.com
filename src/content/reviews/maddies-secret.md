Showgirls and John Waters fans to the front. Just check the nihilism at the door.

Early took one lesson from Verhoeven: ["No one leaves Showgirls caring about the actual material of her secret."](https://www.avclub.com/john-early-interview-dumped-irony-embraced-tropes-maddies-secret)

It's a camp classic in the making, no argument. Garish lighting, unreal sets, the exact texture of a Lifetime problem-of-the-week movie cranked to eleven. But where Waters or Verhoeven would twist the knife, Early refuses. He takes Maddie and her eating disorder completely seriously while the whole thing stays a riot.

![John Early as Maddie, long blond hair and a blue toile top, smiling into the camera behind a counter of lemons and herbs on the GourMaybe set, copper pans and the company's big logo on the white tile behind her.](/images/reviews/maddies-secret/maddie-on-camera-in-the-gourmaybe-kitchen.webp)

Early wrote it, directed it, and plays Maddie himself, in drag, and somewhere in the second half I forgot a man was under the wig at all. ["To me, the joke was never that I was playing a woman,"](https://www.thewrap.com/john-early-maddies-secret-tiff-interview/) he told TheWrap. She isn't a bit. She isn't a victim, and she isn't some flawless little martyr either. She's a person, and you get wrapped up in her even while you're laughing. The proof is the showdown with her mother (Kristen Johnston, tremendous) in the last act. It's a real gut-punch, and it's earned, because the movie spent the whole runtime refusing to condescend to her.

I laughed out loud more than once. Early declaring himself "just an ally" at the dance class wrecked me.

My one hangup is entirely my own baggage. I have a phobic aversion to movies about LA and LA production culture, and this thing is soaking in it: the viral food studios, the influencer grind, the executive producers of "The Boar" (the obvious riff on The Bear). Overrepresented, for me anyway, and I drifted whenever it got deep in the weeds on content creation. But I'll give it this. The food-media angle sharpens the eating-disorder story in a way I didn't see coming. A woman whose whole livelihood is performing joy about food, quietly at war with eating it. That's a cruel, smart engine, even if I couldn't make myself care about the office it runs in.

A movie of the week with a soul. They don't make them like this, and they really don't make them this kind.
