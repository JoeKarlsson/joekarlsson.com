Sicario is [The Silence of the Lambs](/reviews/the-silence-of-the-lambs/) without a Hannibal Lecter.

![Jodie Foster as Clarice Starling in a stone corridor, her visitor badge clipped to her jacket.](/images/reviews/sicario-2015/kate-and-clarice-1.webp 'The Silence of the Lambs · 1991') ![Emily Blunt as Kate Macer in an FBI tactical vest at a crime scene, an armored truck behind her.](/images/reviews/sicario-2015/kate-and-clarice-2.webp 'Sicario · 2015')

_Jodie Foster in MGM's trailer for The Silence of the Lambs, and Emily Blunt in Sicario._

Without a Lecter, the monster is the whole border.

![An aerial shot of the border fence cutting a straight line through the desert.](/images/reviews/sicario-2015/the-border-fence.webp)

I watched Sicario to fill the last gap in my Denis Villeneuve filmography before [Dune: Part Three](https://letterboxd.com/film/dune-part-three/) comes out next month, and it rips. Emily Blunt plays Kate Macer, an FBI agent pulled onto a government task force where nobody will tell her what the job actually is. ||Kate finally learns why she's on it: the CIA can't operate inside US borders without a domestic agency attached. "So that's it. That's why I'm here." She's there as legal cover.||

I'm still a little torn on Sicario, and I think it's because the script indicts and the camera recruits. Taylor Sheridan's script is a scathing indictment of US foreign policy toward the cartels, and Villeneuve and Roger Deakins make that same policy look incredible.

The cartel is a hydra that keeps fighting and grows stronger every time someone cuts off a head. ||Josh Brolin's Matt Graver admits the goal isn't to kill the hydra but to put one cartel back in charge, because "until somebody finds a way to convince 20% of the population to stop snorting and smoking that shit, order's the best we can hope for." The US is just choosing which head stays on.|| It's only gotten more relevant since 2015; in 2025 the US [declared several cartels terrorist organizations and started blowing up alleged drug boats in the Caribbean](https://www.npr.org/2025/09/30/g-s1-91091/trump-bombing-drug-boats-terror).

Villeneuve is a master of building dread, and Deakins shot this, which makes it another banger.

![A line of black SUVs racing along the border highway into Juárez.](/images/reviews/sicario-2015/convoy-into-juarez.webp)

I'm still shocked by how many kinds of light he can handle in one film. The tunnel sequence alone goes from silhouettes against a burning dusk sky

![The team in black silhouette against an orange band of dusk on the horizon.](/images/reviews/sicario-2015/dusk-silhouettes.webp)

to night vision to thermal, and it's absolutely breathtaking.

![Soldiers silhouetted on a ridge through green night vision.](/images/reviews/sicario-2015/night-vision-then-thermal-1.webp) ![The team walking into the tunnel's rock walls, glowing white on a thermal camera.](/images/reviews/sicario-2015/night-vision-then-thermal-2.webp)

It also means everyone looks so fucking cool holding a gun and killing cartel members.

![Benicio del Toro as Alejandro aiming a rifle across a jammed lane of traffic at the border crossing.](/images/reviews/sicario-2015/alejandro-takes-aim.webp)

The script doesn't need that, but at the end of the day it still plays a little like pro-military propaganda, and I still hate that.

The movie knows Kate is the one good cop and uses that on purpose. The cliché still feels like propaganda to me.

Blunt is incredible anyway. Sicario and [Edge of Tomorrow](/reviews/edge-of-tomorrow/) are evidence she should have been the biggest action star of our generation, and it's a shame her action career never went much further than this.

![Emily Blunt as Kate in the back of an SUV in Juárez, staring straight ahead.](/images/reviews/sicario-2015/kate-and-rita-1.webp 'Sicario · 2015') ![Emily Blunt as Rita Vrataski in a battle exosuit, fire burning behind her.](/images/reviews/sicario-2015/kate-and-rita-2.webp 'Edge of Tomorrow · 2014')

_Emily Blunt in Sicario, and in Warner Bros.'s trailer for Edge of Tomorrow._

For me, though, the star of the show is Benicio del Toro as Alejandro. The nearest thing to a Lecter isn't in a cell; he's riding in the same SUV as Kate.

![Benicio del Toro as Alejandro in sunglasses in the passenger seat, watching the road.](/images/reviews/sicario-2015/alejandro-in-the-suv.webp)

He's so good at simmering, bottled-up rage, held down by patience and single-minded determination, and the film makes clear what's driving him. When it finally comes out at the end, it's heartbreaking, stunning, cold and filled with rage. ||Alejandro walking in on Fausto Alarcón's family dinner is Sicario's|| [Kill Bill: Volume 2](https://letterboxd.com/film/kill-bill-vol-2/) ||ending, the long-awaited face-off with the man who took his family.||

![Alejandro and Fausto Alarcón sitting at opposite ends of a long dinner table at night.](/images/reviews/sicario-2015/dinner-face-off-1.webp 'spoiler') ![Bill and Beatrix facing each other across a wooden table in Kill Bill: Volume 2.](/images/reviews/sicario-2015/dinner-face-off-2.webp 'spoiler')

_Sicario, and David Carradine and Uma Thurman in Miramax's trailer for Kill Bill: Volume 2._

||Alejandro's last words to Kate are "You are not a wolf. And this is the land of wolves now." Sicario condemns the system, then hands the wolf the last word with Kate.||

Now that I've finished every Villeneuve film, it's interesting to think about the [James Bond movie he's making for Amazon MGM](https://variety.com/2025/film/news/james-bond-denis-villeneuve-director-1236441253/). I'm sure we'll get a morally gray, deeply conflicted Bond. Personally, I think it's time for Bond to reckon with the evils of the British Empire's foreign policy, and Villeneuve is the right man for the job.
