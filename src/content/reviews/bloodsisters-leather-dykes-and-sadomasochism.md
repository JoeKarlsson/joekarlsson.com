Liberation that only covers the respectable parts of queer life doesn't count, and BloodSisters is 69 minutes of proof.

Shot on Hi-8, BloodSisters spends 69 (noice) minutes with San Francisco's leather dyke scene and lets the women in it do nearly all the talking. Patrick Califia, Skeeter, Tala Brandeis, Queen Cougar and the rest walk you through their world on their own terms: the vocabulary, the rituals, the Ms. Leather pageant, the leather Skeeter makes by hand. No narrator steps in to translate them for a nervous audience. I love talking-head documentaries built this way, because the people on screen get to be the authority on their own lives instead of specimens under somebody else's glass.

What stayed with me is who these women were up against. The religious right, sure. But also a lot of the mainstream lesbian community, which treated them as an embarrassment to be kept out of the parade photos. Straight culture tends to see queer people as one polite, interchangeable group, and sometimes we do that flattening to ourselves, trimming off whoever makes the respectable version harder to sell. Sex is political whether anyone wants it to be, and the sex that gets called deviant is usually the first thing a movement agrees to give up. BloodSisters is a record of the people who refused to be given up. Nobody gets free while somebody else is left behind to make the rest of us look safer, and that includes the darker corners of queer life.

Yes, it's graphic. There's needle play, there's blood, there are long scenes that don't cut away. But Handelman films all of it with care, filtered and layered and cut to a soundtrack of Frightwig, Coil, and Chris & Cosey, and none of it plays like it's trying to rattle you. It plays like documentation, because it is.

It's also incredibly charming. The lo-fi video titles, the punk and industrial score, the haircuts. Every frame is 1995.

That year matters, too. AIDS deaths in the United States peaked in 1995, and here is a film about queer bodies, blood, consent, and pleasure, made right in the middle of it. Watching these women name their desires out loud, on camera, at that moment, took nerve I can only admire.

There are so few films that look at lesbians like this, with curiosity and without a sneer. BloodSisters is a treasure, and I'm glad someone restored it before it disappeared.
