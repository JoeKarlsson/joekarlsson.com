Somewhere between a fetish and a funeral, a numb man finds a pulse.

Juha is a heart surgeon who watched his wife drown, went in after her, and has been emotionally leveled ever since. He stumbles into Mona's dungeon and finds that being choked to the edge of consciousness brings her back to him. What's impressive is how much the film pulls off with barely any dialogue. These two are icebergs of feeling, everything real happening in a held glance or a flinch, and as a nearly wordless romance it works far better than it has any right to.

I usually bristle at movies that frame a kink as scar tissue from trauma. That isn't how desire works for most people, and I hate the reflex to pathologize sexuality. But it works here because the film never treats Juha's masochism as a disease to cure. Asphyxiation isn't his problem, it's his doorway, the one threshold where he gets to see his wife again, and the movie ends with him walking joyfully into the kink instead of healed out of it. That's the difference between pathologizing a desire and honoring one.

The color is the best thing here. Valkeapää and DP Pietari Peltola split the palette: Juha's ordinary life drains to a cold blue-grey, memory glows green, the dungeon burns red, and pink-and-cyan neon flares between scenes. He's colorless everywhere except the two places he can still feel his wife, the green past and the red room where Mona takes his breath. He only has color when he's dying a little. Those recurring points of light read to me like the phosphenes of asphyxiation, the sparks you see blacking out, the threshold where he meets her. The sound design carries as much of the story as the camera does.

Kosonen and Strang are both fearless, and Kosonen gives Mona real interiority in those glances, which is exactly why I wanted more of her. There's a bittersweet melancholy under her surface the film keeps gesturing at and never opens up. She deserved to be the subject, not the mirror.

Two gripes: after the reviews I'd read I expected Audition-brutal and got something gentler, though the tooth extraction scene had me watching through my fingers. And the stalking bothered me, Juha acting like a creep, hunting Mona down after she's said no, and the film handing that a romantic payoff I couldn't buy. But I'm still turning it over, still wanting more of Mona, still seeing that cold blue house and that red room.
