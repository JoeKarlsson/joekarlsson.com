The scandal of The Last Temptation of Christ is that it takes the creed at its word: fully God, and also fully a man.

Martin Scorsese spent about [fifteen years trying to get this made](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Last_Temptation_of_Christ_(film)>). This is a movie by a man who once studied for the priesthood, and it plays like a confession rather than a provocation. He told [Film Comment](https://www.filmcomment.com/article/interview-martin-scorsese-the-last-temptation-of-christ/) in 1988 that he directed Jesus's doubts that way "because my own religious feelings are the same."

I'm an atheist, and I walked out baffled by the outrage. The film opens with a title card saying it isn't based on the Gospels, yet it felt closer to the Jesus I remember from them than most Bible movies get.

![White serif text on black: This film is not based upon the Gospels but upon this fictional exploration of the eternal spiritual conflict.](/images/reviews/the-last-temptation-of-christ/title-card-not-based-on-the-gospels.webp)

The Gospels give you a man who weeps, who begs in Gethsemane for the cup to pass from him, who cries out on the cross that God has forsaken him.

![Jesus at night in Gethsemane, face pressed against his clasped hands in prayer.](/images/reviews/the-last-temptation-of-christ/gethsemane-cup.webp)

Willem Dafoe plays that man.

![Willem Dafoe as Jesus, shirtless in a dim carpenter's workshop, staring wide-eyed and frightened.](/images/reviews/the-last-temptation-of-christ/frightened-carpenter.webp)

His Jesus is frightened and unsure whether the voice in his head belongs to God or to madness, and he is still lit up by something nobody around him can see.

_Dafoe in the opening voiceover, as Jesus builds a cross for the Romans._

He carries the divine around in a body that gets tired and hungry and wants things, and Dafoe never lets either half swallow the other.

The wanting is what got people.

In the long final stretch, Satan offers Jesus a way down off the cross: a wife, children, an ordinary life with all its small comforts.

![On the hill of crosses, a red-haired girl in a plain robe stands beside a bloodied Jesus, who has come down off his cross.](/images/reviews/the-last-temptation-of-christ/angel-leads-him-off-the-cross.webp 'spoiler')

It's the tenderest part of the movie, and apparently the least forgivable. I think the protesters understood exactly what it was saying.

![A newspaper photo outside the Ziegfeld theater in New York: under a marquee reading The Last Temptation of Christ, a woman holds up a crucifix and a sign that says Hollywood's vile insult to Christianity, while an older woman in a sun hat presses her hands together in prayer.](/images/reviews/the-last-temptation-of-christ/ziegfeld-protest-1988.webp)

_Protesters outside the Ziegfeld in New York on opening day, August 12, 1988. Photo: The Greenville News, [public domain](<https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Christians_protesting_The_Last_Temptation_of_Christ_(1988).jpg>)._

A temptation only counts if it tempts you, and Scorsese insists that a quiet life with Mary Magdalene would have tempted Jesus, which is the only way the sacrifice costs him anything.

![Barbara Hershey as Mary Magdalene, in a white dress and a crown of green leaves, looks up at Jesus on a hillside.](/images/reviews/the-last-temptation-of-christ/mary-magdalene-wedding.webp 'spoiler')

I didn't love it. At 164 minutes it drags, and that closing was hard for me to sit through.

Still, I believe Scorsese needed to make this, and I'm glad he did.
