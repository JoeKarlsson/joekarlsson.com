I identify a little too much with the tiny lamb the whole flock refuses to play with.

He was born in winter, when almost every lamb comes in spring, and for reasons George the shepherd says "only make sense to a sheep," that's enough. The grown-ups shoo the other lambs away from him.

![The Winter Lamb standing alone in the long grass next to a metal bucket.](/images/reviews/the-sheep-detectives-1/winter-lamb-alone.webp) ![A big horned ram looming over the spring lambs by George's trailer, the Winter Lamb off to one side.](/images/reviews/the-sheep-detectives-1/flock-shoos-the-lambs.webp)

Every single time that little guy showed up on screen, I lost it.

I think I figured out why he wrecks me. It's hard enough to see someone little, especially a small animal, get rejected that harshly. But this time it felt like being an adult and looking back at feeling rejected as a kid by your family, and still trying to recover from that hurt. The movie has a line for exactly that: "That's what a winter lamb hears, over and over, until one day, he starts to believe it's true."

And George whispers to the lamb that a winter lamb is the best lamb.

![Hugh Jackman as George, cradling the Winter Lamb against his chest in the field, the flock behind him.](/images/reviews/the-sheep-detectives-1/george-holds-the-winter-lamb.webp)

I didn't give the Winter Lamb nearly enough room when I [reviewed this in May](/reviews/the-sheep-detectives/). Six months after the theater, the movie hit a little softer, but I still wept, because seeing animals hurt or grieving or in pain just does it to me.

It's also still refreshing. I've sat through some abysmal kids' movies this year, [The Super Mario Galaxy Movie](/reviews/the-super-mario-galaxy-movie/) among them, and this one treats children with respect. It deals with death and grief and memory head on and trusts kids to handle it. Kyle Balda, the director, [has said he lost his mom when he was really young](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=t5b9QI-SYOk&t=249s), and he wanted the film to give parents a way to talk to their kids about this stuff. "These are hard subjects to talk about," he said.

Knowing who did it, I spent this watch on the color. The characters' outfits keep landing on blue, yellow and green, and the neon signs in town do it too.

![Nicholas Galitzine as Elliot in round glasses and a mustard-yellow tie.](/images/reviews/the-sheep-detectives-1/elliot-yellow-tie.webp) ![Molly Gordon as Rebecca in a bright green cardigan.](/images/reviews/the-sheep-detectives-1/rebecca-green-cardigan.webp)

![Tim and Elliot talking under a green neon sign reading "Nancy's Neon".](/images/reviews/the-sheep-detectives-1/nancys-neon.webp)

I'm not sure what it all adds up to, and the closest Balda has come to explaining it is [saying the colors and costumes were there](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_EmsH0GHDsk&t=227s) to balance the dark stuff with something beautiful. The costumes are planned down to the fit: Nicholas Braun says Tim's uniform was cut to make him look dorky and small, then [a tighter shirt for the big scene at the end](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=U8IMUse4i6c&t=805s).

![Tim early on, his uniform loose on him.](/images/reviews/the-sheep-detectives-1/tim-blue-uniform.webp 'Early on') ![Tim in the finale, addressing the village in a better-fitting shirt.](/images/reviews/the-sheep-detectives-1/tim-finale-shirt.webp 'The finale')

The reveal is still the weakest part of the movie. It's convoluted, but it's also simple, but it's also dumb. I wanted a Knives Out reveal, and it just doesn't do it. [Paddington 2](/reviews/paddington-2-1/) is still the better film because it fucking nails the third act this one struggles with.

But god damn it, I respect this movie so much. It's charming, Nicholas Braun (Cousin Greg forever) still got so many chuckles out of me as the village's one bumbling cop, and it's still one of my favorites of the year. It's underrated, too. If you have kids old enough for the heavy stuff, go watch it with them.

The movie's winter lambs are kids who got pushed away by their own family.

I'll be revisiting it many more times. I think it's wonderful, and winter lambs really are the best lambs.
