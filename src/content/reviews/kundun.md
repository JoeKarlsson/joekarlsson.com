Kundun made me furious at China, furious at Disney, and weirdly unmoved by Kundun.

I'd never heard of it until a few days ago, and there's a reason for that. I'm in the middle of the Blank Check Scorsese miniseries, catching up on everything of his I missed. China objected to this one, Disney opened it in two theaters on Christmas Day and barely pushed it after that, and the next year Michael Eisner flew to Beijing, apologized, and told China's premier the good news was that nobody watched it. He was trying to get a theme park built at the time. Honestly, fuck Michael Eisner.

I get why the Chinese government is pissed, too. They look terrible in this, and they look terrible because they invaded Tibet and committed horrible crimes there.

![Chinese soldiers carrying red flags march past the foot of the Potala Palace.](/images/reviews/kundun/pla-marching-into-lhasa-1951.webp)

_The People's Liberation Army marching into Lhasa, October 1951. Photo: PLA Daily, [public domain](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:PLA_marching_into_Lhasa.jpg)._

It made me enraged all over again. I'd never looked into the plight of Tibet before, and I came out knowing a lot more than I went in with, so the movie did that job.

It's also the least Scorsese-like movie I've ever seen. It's impressionistic and artful, shot by Roger Deakins and scored by Philip Glass. Whole squares of Lhasa fill with monks in red and saffron, so many that the crowd moves like one slow tide under the Potala.

![A procession of hundreds of monks, nobles and townspeople crossing the square below the Potala Palace, with snowy mountains behind.](/images/reviews/kundun/procession-under-the-potala.webp)

The worst of the invasion comes to him in a dream. He stands distraught in a red circle of dead Tibetans, and the camera pulls up and away until there are too many bodies to count and they stop looking like people.

![Two frames: the Dalai Lama standing among the bodies of monks in red robes, then the camera high above a field of red that runs past the edge of the frame.](/images/reviews/kundun/red-circle-of-dead-monks.webp)

The colors and the sheer number of extras reminded me of Kurosawa's Ran. In Ran the red is banners on an army that's still alive. In Kundun it's robes on the dead.

![Two frames: a burning castle in Ran above an army of red and yellow banners, then the field of dead monks in Kundun.](/images/reviews/kundun/ran-then-kundun-in-red.webp)

_Ran's burning castle, from Rialto Pictures' 4K restoration trailer, next to Kundun's field of dead monks._

It does rhyme with The Last Temptation of Christ, though. Both take a spiritual leader and show a man instead of a god, struggling with human feelings and a political situation way too complicated for anyone to win. But Willem Dafoe plays Jesus manic, like a man fighting his own mind. Tenzin Thuthob Tsarong plays a monk, and he rarely lets an emotion out.

![Two frames: Willem Dafoe as Jesus, wide-eyed and shouting at the sky in a crowd, then Tenzin Thuthob Tsarong as the Dalai Lama, eyes lowered, face still.](/images/reviews/kundun/dafoe-manic-tsarong-still.webp)

_Dafoe in the temple in The Last Temptation of Christ, and Tsarong in Kundun._

I think that's intentional, and probably true to life, and it still makes him hard to connect with.

It doesn't help that the Dalai Lama is physically kept away from what's happening. He keeps asking to see, and his handlers keep telling him it isn't for his eyes.

![Monks crowd a doorway with their eyes on the young Dalai Lama, who stands in the foreground with his back to the camera.](/images/reviews/kundun/not-for-your-eyes.webp)

Which means it isn't for ours either. Scorsese keeps putting a telescope in his hands. As a boy he watches Lhasa through one from the Potala roof, and what he sees is guards with rifles on a wall, too far off to hear. ||In the last scene he's still looking through one, at the mountains between him and home.||

![Two frames: the boy Dalai Lama at a brass telescope on the Potala roof, then what he sees through it, guards with rifles on a prison wall far below.](/images/reviews/kundun/telescope-from-the-potala.webp)

We get glimpses in dreams and on the news, filtered through a man who is held at a distance, so I was held at a distance too. By the time he flees across the border into India, I was open to it, and it didn't hit super hard.

![Two pictures: Tsarong in a wool cap and scarf among his armed escort in the film, then a black-and-white photo of the real escape party sitting on a hillside with rifles.](/images/reviews/kundun/escape-film-and-1959.webp)

_The escape in Kundun, and the real Dalai Lama with the party that got him out of Tibet, March 1959. Photo: unknown author, [public domain](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Dalai_Lama_fleeing_Tibet,_March_1959.jpg)._

It's a beautiful film. I respect it. I don't know if I'll ever revisit it.

The Dalai Lama has never been allowed back into Tibet. Shame on Disney for burying this.
