I’m treading carefully with this one given today’s political climate, but I really loved The Weather Underground. I found this after watching and loving [One Battle After Another](/reviews/one-battle-after-another/), and it covers the real-life events of radical Vietnam War-era political activists and the aftermath of their work.

![Top of an FBI wanted poster reading Wanted by FBI, Kathie Boudin, with three 1969 photos of a young woman with long dark hair.](/images/reviews/the-weather-underground/the-fbis-1970-wanted-poster-for.webp "margin: The FBI's 1970 wanted poster for Weather Underground member Kathy Boudin. Wikimedia Commons, public domain.")

This feels like a documentary that shouldn’t be allowed to be aired. I learned a lot about the movement, what worked and what didn’t, and frankly found parts of it inspiring. But the most interesting aspect is watching them deal with the aftermath: going underground, hiding, paying the costs of their choices.

The film was made in 2002, the year after 9/11, and you can feel that weight in every frame. There’s a palpable twinge of guilt as they grapple with having committed acts of political terrorism while the national sentiment toward those acts had completely shifted beneath them. Watching them struggle to find their place in society is genuinely fascinating. I’d love to see an update on where they are today.

I love these early 2000s digital documentaries. They’re charming and feel appropriately indie and accessible for taboo subjects like this.
